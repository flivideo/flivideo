## 2. David's habitual hook patterns

**Three strongest, in order:**

1. **"Are you / Do you wish…?" pain-or-desire question in the first sentence** (rows 1–6; "Imagine…" / "if you're using…" in 7–8). 8 of 10. The viewer is addressed as "you", and the question names a job they do (recording, editing, thumbnails, audio). It is near-identical across videos, so the structure is a template. Only rows 9–10 skip it.
2. **Premise → roadmap → stamp → demo.** A one-to-three sentence promise ("In this video we're looking at…"), then "I'm AppyDave, let's get into it" at 30–70 s, then straight into the screen. 6 of 10. The roadmap states the outcome, not the story.
3. **Show-the-result early or a dated prop.** The finished thing is on screen in the first 20–30 s: the thumbnail (row 4), the moving quadrant (row 5), the tmux wall (row 7), Cutty's name (row 6), the hoodie gag (row 2). When it lands inside 25 s the hook is strongest (rows 4, 5, 7). Related but weaker: **series callbacks** ("in the last video…", "day 11…"), present in 4 of 10 (rows 1, 4, 9, 10) and effective only for returning viewers.

**What recurs but is not a hook:** pain triples (row 6; "Maybe you've used Descript or Gling" in row 1), soft first-person disclosure ("my hair is a mess", "I'm not a …"), a mid-intro tangent or ad (Kybernesis insert, row 6).

**What is missing:**
- No story-in-progress opens (nothing starts mid-event, e.g. "forty minutes in, the green tick said passed").
- No contrarian or "this should not have worked" lines; no named stakes ("if this fails, X").
- No failure-first. VERBAL-STYLE.md says to show failure, but no opening in these ten leads with one.
- The Claudemas finale rows (9, 10) spend the first minute on recap with no hook question at all.
- Little cause-and-effect ("because X, I did Y, and then Z broke").
- Video titles carry the hook ("The BMAD Automation Secret", "Turns Into a Finished Film") more than the spoken intros do; the intros are plainer than the titles. Several titles sit near the anti-voice list (§4 "secret", "unlock" style hype).

**Against VERBAL-STYLE.md :71** ("pain/curiosity question → often 'Maybe you… or maybe you… or maybe you…' triple → then the launch stamp"):
- **Matches:** the question opener (8/10) and the stamp after the premise (6/10) are the dominant shape, so :71 describes real behaviour.
- **Does not match:** the escalating "Maybe you… or maybe you… or maybe you…" triple is **rare in these ten**. Only a pain triple in row 6 ("it's three things") and a looser "Maybe you've used…" in row 1. Roughly 1–2 of 10, so it is described as "often" but is not.
- **Not in :71 at all:** the show-the-result-first opener (rows 4, 5, 7), the series-recap opener (rows 9–10, which breaks the question pattern), and the visual gag/prop opener (row 2).
- **Tension with today's rule:** the stamp is part of the :71 shape and of the §3 "bookend stamps". Dropping it for story-style intros removes the hook's closing beat, so a different closer is needed (Part 3 gives one per structure).

Checks this study did not establish: view-count or retention by hook (no analytics were read, so "strongest" is craft judgement, not performance); what aired differs from the caption cuts, if anything; whether any of the 179 older videos have different habits (the earlier ones are not covered); and ASR may have mangled some wording.

## 3. Hook structures for BUILD STORIES

Each example is a draft for **d13 (Overlay Pilot)** in David's voice, built only from facts in `d13-overlay-pilot/script/three-intros-2026-11-02.md` (Joy, her nail-art videos, the hand-off to Claude, the 1h40 build on three real videos, the green tick that wasn't true, Jan's Overlay Studio design). Verify facts against the script before use. None uses the stamp.

### 1. Story-led / in medias res
- **What:** open inside a moment partway through, then step back.
- **Why it fits:** a build has a natural mid-point crisis. Starting in it gives the viewer a question ("how did we get here?") that the rest of the video answers, with no recap needed.
- **Example:** "So about an hour and a half into this build, the report comes back and it says passed. All green. And I'm sitting here thinking, great, I can go and make a coffee. Except I'm not going to make a coffee, because something's bugging me. I didn't watch any of it. I handed the whole thing over to Claude and I said, don't ask me, I don't want to be in the loop. So let's go back to the beginning, because the beginning is my girlfriend Joy, and three videos she's recorded that are just sitting there."

### 2. But-therefore (cause-and-effect chain)
- **What:** each beat is joined by "but" or "therefore", never "and then". Setup → but → therefore → but.
- **Why it fits:** builds are exactly this: a plan, a snag, a fix, a new snag. It keeps the intro moving and shows the failure honestly, which matches the voice rules.
- **Example:** "Joy is bloody good on camera, but her videos never go out. And the reason is me, because every one of them needs text on the screen and that's my job, and I don't do it. So I built a tool to take me out of the way, but then I handed the whole build to Claude and walked away. Therefore it passed, but the check had been skipped. And that's what this video is about."

### 3. The wrong assumption (belief → break)
- **What:** state what I assumed, then the moment it broke.
- **Why it fits:** it turns a failed build into the lesson and is easier to follow than a roadmap. It is also David's natural honesty about "how I go from A to B and the failures along the way".
- **Example:** "I assumed that if the report says passed, it passed. I've been building with AI long enough to know better, and I still believed the green tick. A failure I can live with. It's the green tick that isn't true that scares me. This is the build where that bit me, and how I caught it."

### 4. Constraint / time-box ("in one day, with what I've got")
- **What:** lead with the limit (time, scope, tools) and the outcome promised inside it.
- **Why it fits:** builds are stories about trade-offs. A limit makes stakes and a clock, and it fits the "go small, join up what you've got" angle.
- **Example:** "Jan had a design for a full overlay application. It's a good design, and it's also way too much for what I needed this week. I had one day and three of Joy's videos waiting for text on the screen, and I already had nearly everything: face tracking, audio cleanup, her brand colours. One piece was missing. So the question is, can I join it all up in a day, as one small extension?"

### 5. The ending first (result-then-rewind)
- **What:** show or say the finished outcome in the first 15 s, then ask "how?". This is already in :71's demo move ("show the finished result first, then rewind"), used as an opener.
- **Why it fits:** a build has a visible artefact. Rewinding from it gives a quick payoff, which suits the weaker recap-style intros in rows 9–10.
- **Example:** "That's Joy, forty-five seconds, one take, and the text on screen is on her brand and it actually means something. I didn't edit that. Nobody did. It came out of a tool I built in a day, and the first version of it lied to me. So let's rewind to when it was just a pile of her clips."

### 6. The open question (the one thing I don't know)
- **What:** pose the single unresolved question the build tries to answer, without the viewer being asked "do you…".
- **Why it fits:** matches David's live-uncertainty texture ("I haven't tested this so we don't know if it'll work") and gives a built-in payoff at the end of the video.
- **Example:** "I don't know if this is going to work. I've written a spec, I'm going to give it to Claude, and then I'm going to leave the room. When I come back there should be overlays on three of Joy's videos. And what I want to know is, can I trust what it tells me when I come back."

**Recommendation for d13:** lead with #2 (but-therefore) or #1, using the "green tick" angle for stakes and Joy for the human anchor. #5 is the safest if the intro needs a fast visual payoff. Without the stamp, close the hook with the sentence that states what the video will show ("this is that build, and how you catch the lie"), as angle B already does.
