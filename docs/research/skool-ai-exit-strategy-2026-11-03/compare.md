## Hook (first 15 s)
- Mark V1: "If you're over 40 and have a corporate job, don't skip this video." Names the viewer by age and situation, then the promise (3-step system, first sales in 60 days), then a fear line about "what happens if they decide they don't need you anymore."
- Jan b28: "If you make YouTube videos with A I, and your views aren't turning into money, don't skip this." Same formula as Mark's, clearly modelled on it. Then "In the next three minutes".
- Mark V2: "Hey, everybody. Mark here. So let's take a quick tour." Casual, no hook (members have already joined).
- Jan b29: "Here's everything inside the A I T L D R school community, in thirty seconds." Stronger than Mark V2: a promise plus a time box.
- Jan b30: "You joined. Now what?" Good and short.

## Structure (beats)
- Mark V1: hook, qualifier ("you don't need to be technical"), name the system, proof (Hugh), the problem ("tried everything"), origin story (satellite office, re-interview, lying awake), more proof (Joel), reframe ("You don't have a tech problem... What you don't have is a system"), metaphor (box of car parts), the 3 parts, proof (Andrej), future-pacing ("Imagine weeks two and three..."), proof (Tyrone), the offer stack with price anchor, guarantee, proof (Richard), two-path close, CTA. Five testimonial clips sit roughly every 90 s.
- Mark V2: tour in app order (community, calendar, classroom left to right, stages 1, 2, 3), each item with a "why it matters" aside. Heavy spoken filler ("Okay? All right?").
- Jan b28: hook, qualifier, who he is, numbers, 3 steps (make faster, get watched, get paid), objections as quoted questions, what's inside, price, CTA. Tight, no filler.
- Jan b29: list first, then tour. b30: five numbered parts with a stated rationale ("Win first. Library second. Slow things last.").

## Voice and person
- Mark: first and second person, very personal ("I didn't tell my wife", "I'd lie in bed at night, staring at the ceiling"). Short sentences, spoken filler in V2. Plain words ("print money", "pre-sell"), little jargon, and terms are explained ("think of a website, but really not a website").
- Jan: "you" and "we", sparse "I". Alex presents as an AI host ("yes, I'm an A I. That's kind of the point"). Very short declaratives; product names act as jargon (Hook Doctor, Title Lab, Claude Code, API keys); letters spelled out for TTS. More technical vocabulary than Mark.
- Story and metaphor: Mark leans heavily on story plus one metaphor (car parts). Jan has none; the nearest thing is "We hand you an order."

## Proof and specifics
- Mark: named people with outcomes (Hugh, "first $1,000 day... three weeks"; Andrej the farmer from Slovenia; Joel, "millions of dollars"; Tyrone the CTO), credentials (five ClickBank Platinum awards, ClickFunnels Two Comma Club), real testimonial clips in their own voices, and a safety line ("I can't promise you results").
- Jan: channel numbers ("over four hundred videos, and nearly four hundred thousand views"), counts ("thirty-one Claude skill lessons"), and deliberately no revenue claim. No member proof or voices (the community is new).

## Call to action
- Mark: price stacked against a $900-$5,000 market, $149/yr option, 30-day refund, month to month, a binary choice ("Path one... Path two"), a reason to act today ("you'll be on my very next call"), and "click the join button on this page right now".
- Jan: "It's five dollars a month." then "Join, open the classroom, and run your first skill today. Then post what you made." Gentle and action-first; no guarantee, no urgency, no two-futures close. b29 ends "The link is below."

## What Mark does that Jan's don't
1. Origin story with emotion (the ceiling at night); the viewer feels seen before being sold.
2. Interleaved human testimonials.
3. Names the real enemy: "You don't have a tech problem... What you don't have is a system."
4. Future-pacing ("Imagine that first sale notification... in the middle of a staff meeting").
5. Risk reversal plus a price anchor, and a two-path close.
6. Repeats one tagline ("Build a paycheck no boss can take away") about six times.

## What Jan does that Mark doesn't
1. Brevity and pace: 2 to 3 min vs 9 to 17; 150-160 wpm vs Mark's ~190.
2. A time-boxed promise; the whole list up front in b29.
3. Objection handling as quoted questions ("Do I need A P I keys?" "No.").
4. A numbered path with a reason for the order (b30).
5. Honest and novel angle: AI host, no invented revenue.

## Suggestions for Jan's versions
1. Add one emotional beat to b28 before the three steps: the owner's pain as a scene, not a category (for example, you post, you get views, and the money page stays empty). Mark's "ceiling at night" does this job.
2. Name the diagnosis like Mark: "You don't have a views problem. You don't have a tools problem. You have no system." Jan lists the cure but never names the disease.
3. Add one proof beat: a named member result, a screen of a real community post, or Jan's own first "Sell it" result. Without it b28 is claims plus counts. Keep the no-revenue rule: a result, not a dollar figure.
4. Close b28 with a short two-path choice and one reason to join today (for example, the next skill drops this week), not only a price. Mark's "Path one / Path two" is cheap to copy.
5. Give b28 and b29 a repeated anchor line, as b30 has ("Win first. Library second. Slow things last."). b28's "Make faster. Get watched. Get paid." is good: say it twice in b29 as well.
6. For David to decide: Jan's biggest gap against Mark is a human voice. With an AI host, consider one real member clip or screen reaction to give the relief Mark's testimonial clips give.
