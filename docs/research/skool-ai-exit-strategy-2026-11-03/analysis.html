A coaching read · Jan · b28, b29, b30

For Jan: bringing b28–b30 up to a proven Skool storefront

Your three scripts are clean and well ordered. They are missing the parts that make a stranger trust a page: a named system, a villain, and one real person.

01

The short version

We took a Skool about page that works (Mark Vaudreuil's AI Exit Strategy) and mapped how it is built, so you can see which pieces your videos already have and which to add.

1

Mark's page is two videos and one image

Slot 1 is a sales video (8:58) that persuades. Slot 2 is a classroom tour (17:27) that reassures. Slot 3 is a diagram image of his system that anyone can take in at a glance. Persuade, reassure, glance.

2

Start Here belongs in the classroom

Mark does not put his Start Here on the about page. Your b30 is a Start Here, so move it to the classroom. Your about page becomes b28, b29 and a diagram image.

3

His sales video follows a fixed order

The system is named by 0:28. A named person speaks about every 90 seconds. An open loop ("one thing nobody tells you") is paid off at 3:43 with a diagnosis. Then a metaphor, a price anchor and a guarantee, and a two-path close.

4

What you already do well

You break everything into three clear parts. In b28 you answer objections as quoted questions. In b30 you explain the order ("Win first. Library second. Slow things last."). Keep all three.

5

Pace is not the problem

Mark speaks at about 190 words a minute, you at about 155, and your voice is synthesized, so that is a setting. Sentence length is a writing choice: yours are shorter, and b29 is almost all very short lines.

6

The gap is trust, not polish

He tells a scene, names the problem, and puts real people on screen. You give counts and steps. Those are things to add, not things to fix.

Your to-do list, in priority order

  1. Name the system (all three videos). "Make faster. Get watched. Get paid." is a slogan; a name is something viewers can repeat and you can draw. This one is to be decided with David before you write anything else.
  2. Add one named proof beat to b28 and one to b29. A member post, a result on screen, or your own first finished "Sell it" job. Use results, not dollar figures.
  3. Name the villain in b28. "You don't have a views problem. You don't have a tools problem. You have no system around the video."
  4. Write one scene for b28 before the three steps: 15 seconds of the viewer's own week. Your honest AI host line is a good opening for it.
  5. Hold one loop open in b28 ("the one step most channels skip") and pay it off at step three.
  6. b29: say why each item matters, one sentence each, as b30 already does for lessons.
  7. Move b30 into the classroom and add a line on how fast a question gets answered.
  8. Make the glance image: one diagram of the named system with its three parts, as slot 3 of the about page.

A ninth idea, once the above is done: close b28 with two short paths and one true reason to join this week. Add a guarantee only if the terms allow one.

Your hooks, before and after

These are drafts in your voice and on your topic. Take what works and make them your own. The figures come from your own scripts.

b28 · Intro (about page, slot 1)
Your first 15 seconds now
If you make YouTube videos with AI, and your views aren't turning into money, don't skip this. In the next three minutes, I'll show you the three-step system we're using to turn an AI channel into income, with Claude doing the busywork.
Draft for you to make your own
You post a video, you get the views, and the page where people could pay you stays empty. I'm Alex, I'm an AI, and I run a channel with over four hundred videos. In the next three minutes I'll show you the three jobs I give Claude so the work gets faster, gets watched, and gets paid for.

Your opening is already good. This version swaps the category ("if you make videos...") for a scene the viewer lives in, keeps your honest AI line, and plants the three jobs as a promise you pay off at the end. Once the system has a name, put it in the second sentence.

b29 · Inside (about page, slot 2)
Your first 15 seconds now
Here's everything inside the AI-TLDR Skool community, in thirty seconds. Then I'll show you around. One membership. Five dollars a month. It opens all of it.
Draft for you to make your own
Five things sit behind one five-dollar membership. I'll name all five in thirty seconds, then open every door so you can see which one is yours.

Keeps your list-first structure. "Five things" gives the viewer a count to track, and "which one is yours" turns the tour into a choice.

b30 · Start Here (classroom)
Your first 15 seconds now
You joined. Now what? This is the Start Here path, and it's built for one thing: your first real result, in your first week.
Draft for you to make your own
Your first week has one job: fix one hook. Everything else in this classroom can wait. Eight short lessons, in this order, and by the end you'll have fixed your own and helped someone else fix theirs.

Replaces a good question with a rule that removes choice, and states the win, the lesson count and the order in the first 15 seconds.
