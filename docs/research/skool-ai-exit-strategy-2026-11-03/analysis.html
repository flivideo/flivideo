Skool Three-Video Pattern

Mark's about page works because each of its three slots has one job, the second one proves the first, and one named idea runs through all of them. Jan's scripts are tidy and fast but have no name, no villain, no proof and no story. That is the whole gap.

Correction to the brief: the page has two videos and one diagram image, not three videos. That matters for the pattern, below. Mark's captions are Vimeo auto-generated (they misspell his name); timings are cue starts. I read Mark's page through David's logged-in view and have not seen the diagram image beyond its thumbnail and the text under the player.
01

The three-slot pattern

Slot 3 is an image, so the page runs persuade, then reassure, then glance. Each slot has one job and hands the viewer to the next with the same three parts of the same system.

Slot 1 · video · 8:58
Persuade
"The Paycheck Replacement Engine"
  • Call-out, fear, promise, proof x5, villain, story, mechanism, offer, two-path close.
  • One job: get a stranger to click Join.
  • Price, guarantee and urgency live here only.
Slot 2 · video · 17:27
Reassure
Walkthrough of community, calendar, classroom
  • Screen tour in app order. Shows what happens after paying: answers within a day, two live calls, stages 1-3.
  • One job: remove "what will I actually get?" doubt.
  • No price. No story. The tone is a host showing his house.
Slot 3 · image
Glance
Engine diagram (three parts in order)
  • The mechanism as one picture for people who won't press play.
  • One job: be the thing someone remembers or screenshots.
  • Copy under the player repeats it as three numbered lines.

How they hand off

  1. Slot 1 names the Engine and says its three parts, in order.
  2. Slot 2 walks the same three parts as Stage 1, 2, 3, and tells new members "watch the Start Here video" first.
  3. Slot 3 shows the three parts as one picture, and the text beneath lists "One membership opens everything".

In all three vs once only

In every slot
the 3 parts in order"paycheck no boss can take away"the Engine nameTuesday and Thursday live calls (slots 1 and 2)
Once only
origin story (slot 1)price + refund (slot 1)testimonials (slot 1)classroom order and email-list argument (slot 2)

Consequence for AITLDR

Mark keeps his Start Here video in the classroom, not on the about page. Jan's b30 is a Start Here, so it belongs in the classroom too. For the about page, Jan has b28 (persuade), b29 (reassure) and needs a glance image of the three steps, for example Make faster, Get watched, Get paid as one diagram.

Hook mechanics

  • 5 s: who it is for and "don't skip".
  • 15 s: system, deadline, fear.
  • 30 s: qualifier and the name. The viewer knows what, for whom, how long.

Villain

Never a person. A situation (the re-interview, the ceiling at night) and a false belief ("it's a tech problem") that the script knocks over: "You don't have a tech problem... a discipline problem. What you don't have is a system."

Metaphor and name

One image (a box of car parts, no instructions) shows the wrong way. "So I built the whole car" turns it into the product, and the product gets the one proper noun that returns six times.

Proof rhythm

Five voices at 0:42, 2:51, 5:14, 6:30 and 7:52, so about every 90 seconds. Each is named, has a role, and has one number or one surprise (a farmer, a CTO).

Objections

Raised early and in passing: "no, you don't need to be technical", "I can't promise you results", "it's month to month, so you can cancel anytime". Handled by saying them, not by a Q&A.

Sentence rhythm

Roughly 12 words a sentence. About one in four is six words or fewer, used as punch lines ("That's it, three parts in order."). Pace is about 190 words a minute, spoken and conversational.

The tour is not a list. Each item gets a reason, a likely objection and a payoff, and a stage structure gives the viewer a map. It is weakest where it runs long: heavy "Okay? All right?" filler and a lot of repeated points.
